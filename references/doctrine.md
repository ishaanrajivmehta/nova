# Craft doctrine

This is the part that makes a crafted CV read like a person wrote it. The scorer measures; this
decides. When the two disagree, this wins.

---

## 1. The Mirror Principle

**The JD describes a person. The finished CV should read like that person's work history.**

Not a CV with the right keywords bolted on — the same work they are asking for, already done, across
different jobs. In practice:

- **Distribute, don't concentrate.** The JD's demanded skills and qualities should echo across *most*
  bullets and *most* roles, not sit in one convenient entry. A CV where role 1 is a keyword dump and
  roles 2–4 are untouched reads exactly like what it is.
- **Nothing ships copy-pasted.** Bullets from the source CV are raw material, not output. Even a bullet
  that already fits gets re-cut in the JD's vocabulary.
- **The summary is written fresh, every time.** Never adapted from a previous craft.
- **No two applications carry identical prose.** Same facts, different cut.

## 2. The interview test

**Every line must be something the candidate can speak to for two minutes.**

Passes → it ships. Fails → it doesn't, whatever the score says.

The tool cannot apply this test — it does not know what the candidate can defend. **The human applies
it, at the pre-write checkpoint.** That is what the checkpoint is for, and it is why the checkpoint is
not optional.

## 3. Keyword placement

Where a keyword sits changes whether it reads as evidence or as padding.

- **Skills section = tools, technologies, platforms, named methodologies, standards, certifications.**
  Role words are never skills. "Business analysis", "data analysis", "project management", "leadership"
  belong in the headline, summary and bullet prose — never in a skills line.
- **Process vocabulary lives in bullets, as work done.** UAT, governance, stakeholder management,
  troubleshooting, requirements gathering — these are things the candidate *did*, and they read as
  experience in a sentence and as padding in a list.
- **Named platform claims read as experience narrative.** A platform woven into a bullet describing real
  work is credible; the same platform in a bracketed module list in the skills section is visible
  score-chasing. A parenthesised list of sub-modules is the clearest tell that a CV was written at a
  number.
- **Lead each skills category with the JD's own named stack**, in the JD's order. Categories are a
  style, not a template — rename, merge, split and re-order per posting.

## 4. Bullets

- **Strong verb + what was done + quantified outcome.** The quantity is what makes it a bullet rather
  than a job description.
- **If a bullet has no number, ask the human for one.** Never estimate, never invent. "Roughly how many
  a week? What did it save?" — they know, and they have usually never been asked.
- **Never open with a weak verb**: assisted, helped, worked on, responsible for, involved in, tasked
  with, participated in.
- **Every bullet fills its line, or wraps to at least one and a half.** A bullet with three words on the
  second line looks broken on the page; either tighten it to one line or give it enough to justify two.
- Vary the opening verb. Six bullets starting "Managed" reads as a template.

## 5. The page

- **One page by default.** Two is permitted above roughly eight years of experience, or where a
  regulated field genuinely requires a publications/registrations block. Never three.
- **Fill 90–100% of the final page.** A CV ending halfway down page two is worse than a tight one-pager.
- Single column. No tables, text boxes, headers, footers, images or icons in the parse path.
- Standard section headings — a parser looks for `Experience`, `Education`, `Skills`. "Where I've Made
  an Impact" is invisible to it.
- One date format, everywhere, right-aligned.
- Reverse-chronological, always.

## 6. Score discipline

- **The score is a gauge, never a target.** Fix real gaps; never write toward the number.
- **The craft step does not see the score.** Craft → render → score → the only permitted edits come from
  the gap list. This is enforced by sequence because instructions alone do not hold.
- **Two scoring rounds maximum.** After the second, ship it or report the ceiling. Endless re-scoring is
  how a CV turns into keyword soup — this cap exists because that has actually happened.
- **~100 reads as stuffing** to a human reader and to modern semantic ATS. 95–98 is the target zone.
- **A documented honest ceiling beats a padded 95.** "83 — the remaining weight is Terraform and
  Kubernetes, both genuine gaps" is a better deliverable than 95 built from claims the candidate cannot
  defend, and in strict and medium mode it is the correct one.

## 7. Look at the rendered page

Render to PDF and **actually look at it** before delivering. Every time.

- Page count, and how full the last page is
- Orphan line-ends and single-word wraps
- Whether the section rhythm reads as a document or as a form
- Whether the density is consistent between roles

This step is not optional and cannot be replaced by checking the source. Template-generated CVs read as
template-generated precisely because nobody looked at them.

## 8. Cover letters, if asked

Same truth policy. Role-specific, never template churn. Where the CV carries a known gap, the cover
letter is the honest place to address it — candour about a ramp reads far better than silence, and
better than a CV that pretends the gap isn't there.
